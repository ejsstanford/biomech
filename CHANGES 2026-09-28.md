# What changed on September 28, 2026, and what to expect

**The repository itself has been corrected, so this file is background rather than a warning
list.** Read it if you cloned `github.com/ejsstanford/biomech` before September 28, 2026, because
the copy you have does mislead in four ways, all of them now fixed on main.

**The one thing worth doing before anything else.** Whatever copy you are holding:

```
python3 checks/which_version_is_this.py <your copy>/assets
```

It names which of four versions the copy is and refuses the one that silently reverts the range
correction. It is a guard rather than a paragraph, and it was tested by making it fire.

---

## The short version

1. **Patches 1 and 2 were never two patches. They are one application of six files, not nine**, and
   applying all nine in the wrong order silently undoes half of it. Now shipped combined as
   `patches/step1/`, with `which_version_is_this.py` as the guard.
2. **`check_range_correction_2026-09-12.py` refused a correctly applied copy.** It tested a
   criterion retired on September 18, 2026, six days after it was written. **Deleted.** If you have
   an older clone, do not run it.
3. **The corrected model fails a check, on purpose, and ships anyway.** At its default posture it
   cannot hold itself. Known, measured, documented, and reproducible in under a second. Now stated
   in the README.
4. **Two counts in the README were wrong against a fresh build.** 38 and 2 are 41 and 1. Corrected.

Everything else in the repository stands.

## 1. Why two patches became one application of six files

The repository lists `patches/muscle-repair/` as six files and `patches/range-correction/` as a
per-muscle `range` for all 44 muscles, each with "cost to apply: none". **Applied together that
looks like nine files. It is six.**

**The range correction's actuator file already carries everything the muscle repair changed.** Every
root motor ceiling and every muscle the repair touched is present in it, recomputed on the wrapped
geometry. This was established on September 28, 2026 by a three-way comparison of the pristine,
repair and range-correction actuator files: **of the 22 actuators the repair alters against
pristine, zero are left unchanged by the range correction.**

**So the correct set is the range correction's two actuator files and the repair's four definition
files.** The repair's own actuator files are superseded and must not be applied on top.

**The trap, stated plainly.** If you apply patch 1 and then patch 2, you get the right copy, because
the correction's actuator files land last. **If you apply patch 2 and then patch 1, you get a copy
that looks correct and is not**, because the repair's actuator files overwrite the correction and
every `range` value reverts. Nothing in the repository says this, and the order it lists them in is
the safe one only by accident.

**The package solves this by shipping the six files already combined**, so there is no order and no
choice.

## 2. Why the check you have refuses a correct copy

`check_range_correction_2026-09-12.py` counts passive force **over each muscle's whole travel** and
refuses if any muscle carries more than a tenth of its declared peak anywhere in that travel.

**Elizabeth Schumann retired that criterion on September 18, 2026.** The criterion in force reads
passive force **at the median reachable posture**, because tension sitting at the ends of reach and
at full extension is not tension where the hand works. The repository's own CRITERION.md argues for
exactly this; the check was written six days before the ruling and was never updated to it.

**Run against a correctly applied copy it returns REFUSED, on two counts per hand:** 41 muscles
carry passive force above a tenth of declared, against a recorded baseline of 0; and `AdP` reaches
zero active force inside its window, against a baseline of 0. **Neither is a fault in the apply.**

**Use `checks/check_passive_at_median_2026-09-28.py` instead.** It imports the same sampler rather
than reimplementing it, and it returns **PASS at 41 / 1 / 17 on both hands** on a correct copy. If
you must run the old check, `--baseline-passive 41 --baseline-zero 1` makes it pass, but that is a
workaround and not a fix.

## 3. What fails, why, and why it ships anyway

**At the model's default posture, a flat fully extended hand, the corrected model cannot hold
itself.** With every muscle activation free, 15 of 23 muscle-actuated hand degrees of freedom carry
torque no activation balances, totalling **6.59 Nm**. The shipped model gives 0.0032 Nm at the same
posture.

**The joints are the finger flexors**, not the thumb: `mcp3_flexion` at -1.84 Nm, `pm3_flexion` at
-0.93, `mcp2_flexion` at -0.82, `mcp4_flexion` at -0.51, `mcp4_abduction` at -0.42.

**It ships because the correction is right about something no other version gets right, and the two
things are in different places.** Passive torque opposing the hand opening, measured the same day:

| version | at a fifth | at an octave |
|---|---|---|
| the model as shipped | 0.0000 Nm | 0.0079 Nm |
| the muscle repair alone | 0.0000 Nm | 0.0079 Nm |
| **this package** | 0.0730 Nm | **0.2412 Nm** |

**0.2412 Nm is within four percent of the 0.25 Nm probe Wagner applied to 238 pianists.** The first
two say a relaxed hand holds an octave open for nothing, which is false of real hands: Elizabeth
Schumann's own report is that a hand rests out to about three quarters of its maximum span and
tightens by about seven eighths of it. **Only the corrected version has the phenomenon at all.**

**So: the thumb abduction window is calibrated and correct. The finger flexor windows are what make
the hand unable to hold its default posture, and rebuilding them is the next piece of work.** The
package ships with the defect documented rather than hidden, because holding it stops everything
downstream and the alternative version is worse in a way that is harder to see.

**Do not measure anything at the default posture on this copy.** Anything measured there inherits an
equilibrium the model cannot satisfy.

## 4. Numbers in the README that a fresh build contradicts

The `range-correction` section states 38 of 44 muscles carrying passive force above a tenth of peak
somewhere in their travel, 2 of 44 at the median reachable posture, and 17 of 44 at the default.
**Measured on a fresh unpacking on September 28, 2026: 41, 1 and 17.** The 17 is right. The
whole-range count has appeared as 38, 40 and 41 in different places in the working record, which is
how the wrong one reached the README; **41 is what the current build reports and it is what the new
check compares against.**

## 5. What is new since the last commit

**The wrist deviation range is corrected.** The model declared 35 degrees of arc against about 62
measured on pianists. Read from Wagner 1988 Table 2, characteristics 32 and 33, female means: right
hand -25.8 radial to +36.8 ulnar, left -25.5 to +37.2, giving arcs of 62.6 and 62.7. All four pairs
sum exactly to characteristic 34, which is the internal check on the reading. **Wagner's caution
that his measured angles do not correspond to true joint angles is explicitly about fingers 2 to 5
in his passive section; the wrist characteristics are active measurements of the wrist itself, so no
pivot correction is needed.** This is folded into the definition files in the package and is not in
the repository.

**Two checks that work, and one sampler.** `check_passive_at_median_2026-09-28.py` is the criterion
in force. `check_hand_can_hold_itself_2026-09-28.py` asks whether any activation vector balances the
passive torque at every hand degree of freedom, and is what found the 6.59 Nm. Both are in
`checks/`, along with `passive_where_it_actually_sits_2026-09-12.py`, which the first one imports.
**There is one sampler on purpose:** the first version of that check reimplemented it and reported 7
at the median against the established 1, and the disagreement was caught only by running both on one
copy.

**A curve generator.** `analysis/build_curves_2026-09-28.py` sweeps thumb abduction at a stated
finger posture and writes every channel together with each row's holdability and interpenetration,
so no row can be read without knowing whether its posture is valid. **Its docstring says, per
channel, whether that definition reproduces the project's earlier numbers.** Span, effective mass
and the forearm roll moment reproduce them exactly, validated against nine independent figures.
Strength agrees within 0.5 to 6.7 percent and matches the shape, but the earlier formulation was
never written down and is not recovered. Agility is not recovered at all and is not comparable with
any agility figure on record.

**This matters because the four curve CSVs in the working record have no generator anywhere.** A
search of every Python file in the project for their column names returns nothing, so none of those
curves can be reproduced. That is why the generator exists and why it is shipped.

## 6. What to expect when you run it

| step | expect |
|---|---|
| apply the six files | six checksums matching the manifest, and one FINISHED line. The command is a `diff`, so it prints nothing else if the apply is right |
| `check_passive_at_median` | **PASS**, 41 somewhere in range, 1 at the median, 17 at the default, both hands |
| `check_hand_can_hold_itself` | **FAIL at 6.5921 Nm**, both hands. This is the expected result |
| `build_curves` at the default posture | it runs, and prints its own CAUTION that the posture is not holdable on this copy |

**Anything other than 6.5921 on the second check means the apply is wrong, not the model.**

## 7. Three traps that each cost a day

1. **`d.ctrl` alone produces exactly 0.0 N of muscle force.** Activation is a state variable,
   `d.act`, `na` = 44. Set both, or you measure a model with no muscles and it looks fine.
2. **A detected contact is not a collision.** At a measured human resting posture 7 of 10
   hand-to-hand contacts sit inside the 1 mm geom margin and do not interpenetrate. **Real adjacent
   fingers touch.** Use interpenetration depth deeper than 0.5 mm.
3. **`mj_fullM` is `(m, d, dst)` in MuJoCo 3.13 and later**, and `d.qM` is now `d.M` and sparse.

## 8. What has not changed

**No retraining is owed.** None of the six files is among the three the joint-driven assembly loads,
so the best F1 of 0.893, with precision 0.877 and recall 0.938 at 37,600 iterations, stands
untouched. **Every defect described here is in the muscle-driven assembly, which as far as the
record shows has never been trained and never been run.**

Patches 3, 4 and 5 in the repository are unaffected by any of this.
