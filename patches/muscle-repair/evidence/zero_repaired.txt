FINDING 2, which does depend on the McFarland comparison.
  17 muscles would leave the force-length window in reality, on
  McFarland's optimal fibre lengths, at postures where the model keeps a healthy
  fraction of peak force. For those the model understates the cost of the posture.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE STUDY'S QUESTION.
  Activation is force demanded over force available. A muscle whose available
  force collapses at an extreme posture will report high activation there for a
  reason that is arithmetic rather than physiological, and a muscle whose
  available force never changes will report activation that carries no posture
  information at all. The model as built does both, in different muscles. So the
  answer to the question in the title is: not yet, and not uniformly.

HOW TO READ THE TABLE. A declared loss near 1.0 means the model gives that muscle
almost the same force everywhere in its range. A true loss much larger than the
declared loss means the real muscle would weaken where the model does not.
'to zero' means active force reaches essentially nothing somewhere in range, and
a ratio against it would not be a meaningful number.

CAVEATS THAT MATTER FOR HOW FAR THIS CAN BE PUSHED.
1. The sweep takes joints to their declared limits independently, ignoring
   self-collision and ignoring whether a hand can adopt the combination. It is
   an upper bound on excursion, so the losses are upper bounds too.
2. McFarland's L0 and tendon slack belong to McFarland's tendon routing. Where
   the two models route a muscle differently, notably APB and AdP, the true
   normalised length computed here is not McFarland's and is not this model's
   either. Those rows are indicative only.
3. Eleven of the mappings are inferences from naming, marked with a star. The
   model files do not say what RI, UI_UB and LU_RB represent.
4. This says nothing about whether a trained policy visits these postures. A
   muscle can be badly parameterised at an extreme the task never reaches.
   Answering that needs a trained policy and the logged joint trajectories.
